Minitaure Wargaming

Miniature wargaming is one of, if not my favorite hobby. I primarily play Warhammer 40,000 and Warhammer: The Horus Heresy, however I also try other gaming systems from time to time.

I collect lots of different types of miniatures, and currently I have two partially-finished armies. The first is the Imperial Guard, I like the modern and historical military aesthetic combined with sci-fi technology, and of course the heavy tanks they utilize! The other army I collect is the Salamanders Legion. Besides my favorite color being green, I like the knightly look of their armor and the flame and magma detailing it's embellished with.

My favorite army to paint is definitely the Salamanders, as the flame detailing is a fun challenge to paint, and I've attempted more technical detailing on them. When I first started painting my Salamanders miniatures I was getting more into the "grimdark" side of the setting, and I wanted to make dirty and worn looking miniatures, instead of the more common 'clean' painting schemes. I've challenged myself by adding grime and blood effects, attempting battle damage on armor and vehicles, and kitbashing parts to make heavy weapons on infantry.

My favorite aspects of the hobby are painting, playing, and kitbashing, but if I had to pick one it would be kitbashing. I love taking parts from many different kits and using them to customize my miniautres, or create entirely new ones! One of my favorite kitbashing projects is a tank I scratch-built from two extra turrets, a scale model kit, and assorted detailing parts. My second favorite kitbashing projet is a "diorama" miniature of two soldiers planting a flag into an alien, which I built entirely from spare parts as well!

I always enjoy meeting up with friends to play miniature wargames. I play regularly with my friends and my brother, and regardless of who wins, I always have fun. I enjoy the strategic aspect of creating an army list and thinking of how to utilize it in the battle. Every time I play I get new ideas on how I can counter what my friends do and new ideas for army lists that would be fun to try, which in turn makes me want to play more!

Cosplay

I started making intricate halloween costumes in 7th grade and have enjoyed cosplaying and costume-making ever since!

In seventh grade, I wanted to go as an Imperial Guardsman from Warhammer 40,000 for halloween. Considering that there were no pre-made costumes available, let alone any that would fit me at the time, I had to make my own. Me and my mother worked on the costume, which involved painting a helmet, making a custom chestplate out of EVA foam, and finding khaki clothes I could wear. This costume was not only the first costume I had made myself, but it was a door into a hobby that I continue to enjoy today, and can bond with my mother over. As a former costume designer and founder of a local skating performance company, my mother has been a great help in making costumes and an amazing partner to work with.

In eighth grade, I decided I wanted to go as an armored knight for halloween. Using the lessons we had learned from the previous year, me and my mother worked together to make an EVA foam chestplate and boot covers. The costume wasn't too different from the work we had done when I was in seventh grade, but it was the first time either of us had used foam to make a standalone piece, instead of an attachment to a prexisting costume piece.

In my freshman year of highschool, I decided to challenge myself and create a helldiver costume for halloween. Just like the guardsman, there were no premade costumes at the time, so me and my mother had to make every piece individually with foam. The most difficult part of the costume was the helmet, which was made by combining a cut-out printed template with a custom-made faceplate, detailing, and visor. The chestplate was made from a modified cut-out template, and my mother helped me sew the yellpw trim on to the jacket.

The next halloween, I wanted to improve and redo the Imperial Guardsman costume I had done all the way back in seventh grade. I had grown out of almost all of the costume, and had reused the shoulder pads for the helldiver costume, so I had to make a lot of new costumepieces. The helmet was utilized from the original costume, however I painted helmet graffiti onto it to add more detail. I had to redesign the chestplate with my own template that I designed to fit me, and my mother helped me with the idea to extend the base vest to be wider with fabric, and I traced new shoulder pad templates from the helldiver costume's shoulder pads. I also wanted to add a radio backpack to the costume, which I made from a plastic box with foam and fabric details. I also created my own gas mask template for the costume, although the hose material I used was too inflexible, making it too uncomfortable to wear, however it still looked nice as an accessory piece! I think this Imperial Guardsman costume is one of my favorite costumes that I've made so far. I think it turned out looked as detailed as I wanted it to, and was a fun throwback to the costume that started it all!

In sophomore year, my parents gifted me tickets to PAX East for Christmas. It's not required to cosplay to attend, but seeing as I enjoy costume-making and had an idea, I decided to make a cosplay for the convention. I decided to cosplay as a Death Korpsman, also from Warhammer 40,000. I managed to find the perfect gray trenchcoat for the cosplay at a military surplus store. I purchased a helmet online and modified it to have a crest and a skull emblem as seen on the miniatures. Using my skill from the gas mask I made for halloween, I made a larger gas mask template and found a different hose material. The hose material I decided on was real gas mask hose, so it needed modification to be able to attach to my plastic and fabric gas mask. The hose worked much better than the one for halloween, and I enjoyed cosplaying it at the convention, and I even met another Death Korps cosplayer! The only issue I had with the cosplay was that the mask made it incredibly difficult for others to hear what I was saying, which I think could be addressed by adding small holes in the mask where they wouldn't be visible.

Weightlifting

I started weightlifting in 9th grade and do so to this day.

In eighth grade one of my closest friends started weightlifting, which prompted me to be curious about it. He suggested I try it, and at the start of highschool I started weighlifting at my school's weight room. My friend helped me learn basic routines, different exercises, and helped me build confidence lifting. Eventually, the school's weightroom was feeling too limited, and my friend suggested I look into a membership at his gym. I purchased a membership to the local YMCA and it's where I've weightlifted ever since.

At the gym, I've met new people that have supported me, found confidence in my abilities, and built a perservering mindset of growth through continuous small improvements.
